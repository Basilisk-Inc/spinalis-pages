Compliance as an output of the engineering process.

Regulated European organisations will customise their own AI models on their own hardware. Under the EU AI Act, many of them become the legally accountable provider of what they build. SPINALIS is the platform that builds those models — and records how each one was made, as it happens.

02Why now

December 2027

Binding obligations for Annex III high-risk systems apply from December 2027. Procurement in regulated organisations takes 12–24 months — those decisions are being made now.

The data cannot leave

Finance, insurance, defence and public administration may not send proprietary or personal data to third-country cloud AI — by data-protection law, sectoral rules, national security or internal classification.

A sovereign cloud is not the answer on its own

It keeps data in the Union and may satisfy part of financial services. It does not enter an air-gapped network, does not meet a no-egress policy, and does not produce the evidence.

03Product and proof

Built, validated, and shown working end to end.

One installation, the whole lifecycle

Ingest, synthesise, train, verify, sign off, serve and monitor on the customer's hardware. Every stage writes to one append-only, hash-chained audit journal; Annex IV and Article 27 documents are generated from it.

Any ML or AI task, one platform

Language, documents, search over data, vision, speech, tables — without task-specific code. Three unrelated tasks are measured end to end, with their fine print, on the customer page.

TRL 5 complete

Validated in a relevant environment: customer-class hardware, air-gapped, separated roles, human sign-off before deployment and an immutable audit journal.

Execution-grounded verification

Outputs are executed against a governed data snapshot and compared by result, not by textual similarity — the same primitive measures quality, filters synthetic training data and constrains what a deployed endpoint may return.

04Business model

Priced per governed AI system — the unit of regulatory exposure.

A governed AI system is one solution promoted into production under governance: one entry in the register of AI systems an organisation must maintain. GPU count measures computation, which is commoditised; the number of governed systems measures regulatory exposure. Versions, retraining and redundant replicas are not counted.

The one that matters is the first row. A capable bank assembles the training stack in a fortnight. The governance layer — sign-off, tamper-evident audit, lineage, reproducible builds, generated technical files, revalidation — is several engineer-years, shows no visible capability, and is the part that never gets built.

GRC platforms sit above the engineering process and collect assertions typed by people; we sit inside it and derive the artefacts from execution. Compatible, not competing. Local runtimes are complementary too — we export to the formats they serve.

The moat

Procurable where copyleft is not

A proprietary licence under EU law on a permissive-only stack (Apache-2.0, MIT, BSD). Faster copyleft kernels are given up deliberately, so the product clears procurement.

Evidence that compounds

Audit chain, lineage and revalidation history accumulate inside the customer's perimeter for years. Every record exports in open formats — but the continuous, verified history grows with every build.

Offline by construction

Licences verified offline against machine fingerprints, no telemetry, releases carry their own integrity check. Built for air-gapped sites from day one, not retrofitted.

Regulator-agnostic

Swappable profile packs map one engineering record onto a given regime: the EU AI Act first, ISO/IEC 42001 and the NIST AI RMF through the same mechanism.
